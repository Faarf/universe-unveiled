The Moon

Distance from the Earth: 384,400 km
Diameter: 3474 km
The moon makes one rotation around the earth every 27.3 days.
It is tidaly locked to the earth, which is why we only see one side of it at all times.
The moon has a lot of visible craters because unlike Earth, it has no geological activity.
The moon has no atmosphere.

Mercury

Distance (Sun): 58 million km
Diameter: 4879 km
A year on Mercury is 88 Earth days, the shortest year out of any planet.
A day though on Mercury is 176 Earth days, meaning it takes two years for one day to pass.
The average surface temperature is 167 degrees Celsius, the second hottest before Venus.
Mercury has no moons or atmosphere.

Venus

Distance (Sun): 108 million km
Diameter: 12,104 km
A year on Venus is 225 Earths days.
A day on Venus is 243 Earth days, so its day is longer than its year.
Venus is the hottest planet in the solar system with an average surface temperature of 464 degrees Celsius.
The atmopsheric pressure is 92 times more than on Earth.
It is assumed that Venus once harbored a lot of oceans, but over time as the Sun has grown in strength, the oceans have evaporated, leaving a thick atmopshere trapping the heat from escaping.
Venus has no moons.

Mars

Distance (Sun): 228 million km
Diameter: 6792 km
A year on Mars is 687 Earth days, almost double an Earth year.
A day is 24.7 hours long, just slighly longer than an Earth day.
A Mars day is known as one "Sol".
Summers on Mars can get up to 20 degrees celcius, but winters can drop as low as below -120 degrees Celsius.
Mars has an atmopshere but its over 100 times thinner than Earth's.
Mars has two moons, Phobos and Deimos.

Jupiter

Distance (Sun): 779 million km
Diameter: 142,984 km
A year on Jupiter is 11.8 Earth years.
A day on Jupiter is the shortest in the solar system, only 9.9 hours long.
This fast rotation is what causes the enormous storms all throughout the planet.
Jupiter is a gas giant mostly made out of hydrogen and helium.
Jupiter has 95 moons. Its four largest moons, Io, Europa, Ganymede, and Callisto are known as the Gallilean moons.

Saturn

Distance (Sun): 1.4 billion km
Diameter: 120,536 km
A year on Saturn is 29 Earth years.
A day on Saturn is the second shortest in the solar system, only 10.7 hours long.
Saturn's rings are generally 10 meters thick and are made out of ice and rock.
Saturn has the most amount of moons in the solar system, a whopping 146 moons.
Saturn's largest moon Titan, is the second largest in the solar system.
Titan even has an atmosphere denser than Earth's and is bigger than Mercury.

Uranus

Distance (Sun): 2.9 billion km
Diameter: 51,118 km
A year on Uranus is 84 Earth years.
A day on Uranus is 17.2 hours long.
Uranus is one of two ice giants in the solar system.
Uranus have rings, but they are very faint.
Uranus has 28 moons.

Neptune

Distance (Sun): 4.5 billion km
Diameter: 49,528 km
A year on Neptune is 164 Earth years.
A day on Neptune is 16.1 hours long.
Neptune is one of two ice giants in the solar system.
Neptune has rings but they are very faint.
Neptune has 16 moons.

Pluto

Distance (Sun): 5.9 billion km
Diameter: 2376 km
A year on Pluto is 248 Earth years.
A day on Pluto is 6 Earth days.
The average temperature on Pluto is -232 degrees Celsius.
There are seven moons in the solar system larger than Pluto, including our own moon.
Its located in the Kuiper Belt along with a lot of other icy bodies.
Pluto is a dwarf planet.
Pluto has 5 moons.
